Threat-Intel RAG: Cited Q&A over MITRE ATT&CK

Ask MITRE ATT&CK in English or Chinese and get an answer where every sentence cites its source, or a clear refusal when the sources can't back one.

Features

  • Ask about any ATT&CK technique in English or Chinese. Every sentence links to the passage it came from, and each source opens on the official ATT&CK site.
  • When the sources can't back an answer, it says so instead of guessing: off-topic questions, questions about threat groups, or data ATT&CK doesn't hold.
  • Shows its work: how each answer was found, a judge's verdict on every recorded sentence, and the failures and attacks it doesn't stop.

Why I built it

I wanted to understand security better, and I learn best by building something real. MITRE ATT&CK turned out to be ideal material: it's the shared language defenders use to describe attacker behaviour, it's public and well structured, and its look-alike technique IDs make search genuinely hard. Working with it meant reading it closely, and it let me take on a question I care about: can an AI assistant be trusted to say only what its sources support, and how would you prove it?

Core technical

  • Raised top-5 retrieval accuracy from 47% to 83% with hybrid search (BM25 plus multilingual embeddings) and query understanding that rewrites retired IDs and routes named techniques.
  • Made every sentence cite its source with structured output and a citation check. An LLM judge, validated against my own blind labels (κ 0.87), found 99.2% of sentences fully supported; 24 of 25 out-of-scope questions were refused.
  • Tested prompt injection and RAG poisoning on isolated copies of the index: successful attacks fell from 39 of 70 with free-form answers to 7 with structured output, and to at most 2 with defenses across two runs.
  • Shipped it on Azure Container Apps with a GitHub Actions pipeline that signs in with OIDC (no stored secrets), smoke-tests every release and rolls back on failure; 209 unit tests and 13 browser tests.
